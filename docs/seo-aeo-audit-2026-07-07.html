1 · The mental model

Think of your site as a store with two kinds of shoppers:

Googlebot is a meticulous accountant. It doesn't read your site the way a human does — it reads signals: the URL, the <title>, the meta description, canonical tags, structured data, redirect status codes. When two signals disagree (say, your sitemap says mikareyes.com but the server says "actually, go to www.mikareyes.com"), the accountant doesn't pick a side — it discounts both. Consistency is the whole game.

AI answer engines (ChatGPT, Claude, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews) are more like a researcher on deadline. They skim for extractable answers: clear question-shaped headings, direct answers in the first sentence under each heading, an author with verifiable credentials, dates, and machine-readable summaries (schema, llms.txt). AEO = making your pages the easiest source to quote.

The compounding insightEvery fix below is a one-time systems fix or a template fix. Because your guides are generated by skills, one line added to a skill improves every future page forever. That's your unfair advantage — most creators fix pages one at a time.

2 · Critical issues

Fix first — these suppress everything else

Critical #1

Your site lives at two addresses, and they disagree

Vercel domain config · src/lib/site.ts · every generated URL

The live site serves from www.mikareyes.com. But SITE_URL in your code is https://mikareyes.com (no www). So your sitemap, Open Graph URLs, structured data, robots.txt and llms.txt all advertise apex URLs — and every single one of them answers with a redirect to www.

Evidence: curl -I https://mikareyes.com/robots.txt → 307 → https://www.mikareyes.com/robots.txt. The sitemap at www lists only apex URLs. site.ts:1-2 hardcodes the apex fallback.
The principle: one canonical hostGoogle treats www.mikareyes.com/ai/x and mikareyes.com/ai/x as different pages. Links, shares, and ranking signals get split between them. A sitemap full of URLs that redirect is a sitemap Google partially ignores ("submitted URL redirects" warnings in Search Console). Pick ONE host and make every signal — sitemap, canonical, schema, internal links — say the same thing.

Decision you need to make: apex (mikareyes.com) or www. Apex is cleaner for a personal brand and matches everything you've shared socially. On Vercel: set mikareyes.com as the primary domain so www redirects to apex, not the other way around.

Prompt for Claude

Now audit the repo for host consistency: confirm SITE_URL resolves to https://mikareyes.com everywhere (site.ts, env vars in Vercel, llms.txt, any hardcoded www URLs), and verify after deploy that https://www.mikareyes.com/anything 308-redirects to apex and that sitemap.xml, robots.txt and og:url all use apex. Critical #2

No canonical tag on any page

src/app/layout.tsx · every page's generateMetadata

Not a single page emits <link rel="canonical">. Combined with #1 (two hosts) and query-param URLs like /ai?tag=claude, Google has no authoritative statement of "this is the one true URL for this content."

The principle: the canonical tag is your voteDuplicate URLs are inevitable (www vs apex, ?tag= filters, trailing slashes, UTM params from your IG bio links!). The canonical tag tells Google which version accumulates the ranking credit. Without it, every UTM-tagged share from Instagram creates a competing URL. In Next.js this is one line: alternates: { canonical: url } in each page's metadata.

Critical #3

og:url says "homepage" on every single page

src/lib/site-metadata.ts:23 — url: options.url ?? SITE_URL

Critical #4

Every blog post (188), press item (~40), and project (~20) literally duplicates the title into the description tag. Your AI guides do this right (they have a real description field) — which proves the pattern works; it just was never extended to the other three content types. Evidence: content/blog/*.mdx frontmatter has title, slug, dates, tags — no description. Guides in content/ai/*.mdx have proper 140–160 char descriptions. 74 guides ✓, 188 blog posts ✗.
The principle: the meta description is your ad copyGoogle shows ~155 characters under your title in results. Formula for a strong one: [keyword or most specific claim] — [what the reader gets] (140–160 chars). It doesn't change rankings directly, but it changes click-through rate, which does.

Critical #5

Permanent moves using temporary redirects

/dreams → /my-dreams responds 307 · apex→www is also 307

/dreams redirects to /my-dreams with a 307 Temporary Redirect (it's a redirect() call in a page component, not a config-level redirect). Your llms.txt even documents this as a permanent canonical move. Same for the domain-level redirect.

The principle: 308 transfers the equity, 307 hoards itA permanent redirect (301/308) tells Google "move all accumulated ranking signals to the new URL." A temporary one (302/307) says "keep indexing the old URL, this move might be undone" — so the old URL keeps ranking and the new one starts from zero. Rule of thumb: if you'd put it in your llms.txt as "the canonical target," it must be a permanent redirect.
Prompt for Claude

In mika-website: find every route-level redirect() call used for a permanent URL move (start with src/app/(site)/dreams/page.tsx and my-dreams; check /more → /projects too) and convert them to permanent redirects — either entries in urlRedirects (next.config redirects, permanent: true → 308) or redirect with RedirectType.replace per the Next 16 docs in node_modules/next/dist/docs. Then verify with curl -I that /dreams and /more return 308.

AEO #16

Repurposing loop: social content → search-shaped titles

Titles like "3 Fun Things to Build in Fable Before July 8" — great hooks, expiring queries

Some guide titles inherit their IG hook framing, which is optimized for scroll-stopping, not searching. Date-anchored titles ("before July 8") stop earning traffic after the date passes. The fix isn't to abandon hooks — it's to give each piece two lives: hook framing on social, query framing on the site.

The principle: hooks expire, queries compoundAn IG hook competes for 1.5 seconds of attention today. A guide title competes with a search query forever. When your skill repurposes a reel into a guide, it should re-title for the evergreen query ("Fun Projects to Build with Claude Fable 5") and mention the time-sensitive angle in the intro instead. Small skill change, permanent traffic difference.

Fix lives in the playbook below — it's a rule for your guide skills, not a code change.

5 · The go-forward playbook

Rules to bake into generate-guide-generic, generate-guide-prompt, and any future blog skill — so every page ships SEO/AEO-complete by default.

The frontmatter contract (every new guide/post)

---
title: "How to X in Y"        # ≤60 chars, keyword-first, matches a real query
description: "..."             # 140–160 chars: keyword/claim + what reader gets
published: "YYYY-MM-DD"
updated: "YYYY-MM-DD"          # bump when meaningfully edited
tags: [claude, beginner]       # from a controlled vocab — no one-off tags
faq:                           # 3–4 real questions, answer-first
  - q: "..."
    a: "..."
---

Writing rules that serve both SEO and AEO

  • Title = query, hook = intro. Title matches what someone would search or ask an AI; the scroll-stopping hook goes in the opening paragraph.
  • Answer in the first 2 sentences. After the H1, state what the guide does and for whom before any story. (Answer engines quote openings.)
  • Question-shaped H2s where natural. "Which effort level should I use?" beats "Levels overview." First sentence under each H2 = standalone answer.
  • Every image has search-query alt text. "Claude effort level selector showing five levels" — never empty, never "screenshot."
  • 3+ internal links per guide to related guides, plus one link up to the topic hub once hubs exist.
  • No date-anchored titles. Deadlines and news pegs go in the intro; titles stay evergreen.
  • Slug = title keywords, short, no dates. (You already do this well — keep it.)
  • Cite sources with real links. AI engines trust pages that link out to primary sources; it also earns you entity association.
  • Append new guide to llms-full.txt as the skill's final publishing step.
