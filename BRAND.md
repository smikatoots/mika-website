# Mika Reyes

> Durable identity for the Mika Reyes creator brand: mikareyes.com, the
> presentation decks, and every piece of first-person content on Instagram,
> TikTok, LinkedIn, X, and the newsletter.
>
> This file owns what survives a redesign. The applied visual systems live in
> per-surface `DESIGN.md` files, each of which declares this file with a
> `brand:` path. The link runs one way on purpose: a list of surfaces here would
> go stale the moment one is added or renamed, so find them by walking the
> directory tree for `DESIGN.md` files whose `brand:` resolves to this file.
>
> King's Cross Labs is a **separate brand** with its own voice. Nothing here
> applies to KCL company comms. See `company-brain/core/voice.md` for those.

## Strategy

### Overview

Mika Reyes teaches ambitious high achievers how to use AI to buy back their
time. Not AI news, not AI theory: the specific workflows, prompts, and systems
that take a task from three hours to twenty minutes, taught by someone who runs
them daily on her own business.

The origin is a reversal. Mika did the prestige track all the way to the end,
came to the US from the Philippines on a full scholarship, went through LinkedIn
product, then founded and sold a venture-backed stablecoin payments company, and
found the math at the top didn't add up to the life she wanted. She rebuilt on
the other side: bootstrapped, two people, optimizing for freedom instead of
headcount.

What she really does is give people permission plus a method. The permission
comes from having walked the prestige path far enough to say credibly that it
isn't the only one. The method is AI, taught concretely enough that a
non-technical person can run it the same evening.

The structural problem: the people most capable of using AI to escape the
treadmill are the ones with the least time to learn it, and the material aimed
at them is either hype with no instructions or documentation written for
engineers. Both leave a competent, busy person exactly where they started.

**Transformation.** Before: excellent at her craft, quietly exhausted, watching
AI change her field faster than she can keep up, suspecting she has to choose
between ambition and a life. After: AI-native at her craft, hours back in her
week, and the earned belief that she can have both.

**Long-term ambition:** make AI genuinely usable by people without technical
backgrounds, so the next generation of founders and creators doesn't need a
feeder school or a network to start.

### Audience

**Primary audience: Sam.** A 33-year-old senior growth marketer in NYC at a
recognizable tech company, roughly $250K total comp, first-generation immigrant
who arrived on a full ride and got onto the golden path on merit rather than
connections. Genuinely excellent at B2B growth. Burnt out twice. She is trying
to become AI-native at her craft and build enough optionality to consult, found
something, or stay employed by choice instead of fear.

**What she already believes:** that she is good at her job; that AI matters and
she is behind on it; that the prestige track stopped paying her back somewhere
around the second burnout.

**What she is skeptical of:** AI gurus with no operating history, anyone
promising passive income, tool recommendations from people who clearly haven't
used the tool, and any advice that requires quitting her job on Monday. She has
tried ChatGPT and Claude, has stitched together a few real workflows herself,
and can tell instantly when someone is describing a feature they've only read
about.

**What scares her:** falling behind on AI while the skills she's excellent at
get automated. Betting on the wrong tools. Waking up at forty having spent her
best years on someone else's scoreboard.

**Secondary audiences.** Founders and business owners who want the same
workflows applied to a company rather than a career, and who care about output
per person rather than headcount. Career professionals earlier than Sam, who
need the beginner rung of the same ladder. Both get the same material; the
framing shifts from "your career" to "your company" or "your first step."

**Explicitly not for:** people looking for AI news and model-release commentary,
engineers wanting depth on implementation, anyone shopping for get-rich-quick
or passive-income content, and audiences who want motivation without a method.

**Gut-check for anything produced under this brand:** would this make Sam nod,
save, or share? If not, it doesn't ship.

### Positioning

**Category:** practical AI workflows for non-technical high achievers who want
their time back.

**What it is NOT.** Not AI news. Not model commentary or benchmark analysis. Not
a prompt-pack store. Not motivational career content. Not a coding channel. Not
an agency, and not a course business dressed up as a creator brand.

**Competitive landscape.** The market splits three ways and leaves a gap in the
middle. AI-news accounts move fast and teach nothing you can run. Technical
educators teach things you can run but assume a developer on the other end.
Career and FIRE creators sell the destination with no vehicle. This brand sits
in the middle: the vehicle, explained to a competent non-engineer, pointed at a
destination worth wanting.

**Structural differentials.**

- **Operator, not commentator.** Every workflow taught is one actually run on a
  real bootstrapped business, with the real numbers attached.
- **Exit credibility, bootstrapped conviction.** Raised roughly $5M, scaled past
  $100M in volume, sold the company, then chose the other path on purpose. The
  critique of the venture model is lived, not theoretical.
- **The freedom line is always drawn.** Other people stop at "here's the AI
  move." This brand continues to "here's the time it buys you."
- **Built for the non-technical.** The default assumption is no CS degree, no
  engineer to ask, and a full-time job already in progress.
- **Immigrant, first-generation, no inherited network.** The audience that most
  needs to believe access is possible sees someone who got there without it.

**Territory owned:** *time-rich*. The idea that ambition and freedom are not a
trade, and that AI is what finally makes holding both realistic.

### Personality

**Dominant archetype:** The Generous Insider. The friend who is already inside
the room, took notes, and is texting you what actually happened.

**Brand attributes:** Direct. Generous. Data-forward. Warm. Practical. Free.

**What it IS:** Specific. Honest about what didn't work. Excited to hand things
over. Unimpressed by hype. Fun without being unserious.

**What it is NOT:** Not a guru. Not a hustle account. Not corporate. Not
gatekeeping. Not doom-forecasting. Not performatively humble.

### References & Anti-References

#### References

- **Stratechery and good analyst writing.** Borrow the data-forward confidence
  and the willingness to state a spiky position and then show the reasoning that
  earns it. Do not borrow the density, the assumed context, or the paywalled
  insider register that shuts out the person who most needs the argument.
- **A well-made cookbook.** Borrow the assumption that the reader will actually
  do the thing tonight: numbered steps, exact quantities, photographs of what it
  should look like at each stage, and honesty about which step people get wrong.
  Do not borrow the aspirational lifestyle photography or the long personal
  preamble before the recipe.
- **Duolingo.** Borrow the refusal to be intimidating, the emoji-forward
  friendliness, and the belief that a serious skill can be taught by something
  that feels light. Do not borrow the mascot chaos, the meme-native voice, or
  the gamification-as-manipulation.
- **Linear.** Borrow the restraint, the information hierarchy, and the sense
  that every element on screen was decided rather than defaulted. Do not borrow
  the dark-mode software register or the vocabulary of the developer-tools
  category.

#### Anti-References

- **AI hype accounts and AI guru content.** Reject "X just killed Y" headlines,
  screenshots of a prompt presented as a revelation, thread formatting used to
  stretch one idea across twelve posts, and any claim about what percentage of
  people are "using AI wrong."
- **Hustle-culture founder content.** Reject 5am-grind aesthetics, suffering
  framed as virtue, revenue screenshots as personality, and the implication that
  wanting a life is a lack of ambition.
- **Enterprise SaaS and corporate AI marketing.** Reject "unlock your
  potential," "empower your team," "transformative," gradient-on-gradient hero
  sections, and stock photography of diverse teams pointing at a laptop.

### Promise

**Core promise.**

- You will leave with something you can run today.
- Nothing recommended here is untested. If it didn't work, you'll hear that too.
- No prerequisite degree, no engineer required, no gatekeeping.
- Every AI move connects back to the time it gives you.

**Base message.** AI is the fastest way for an ambitious person to buy back
their time, and it is learnable by someone who is not technical and already
busy.

**Synthesizing phrase.** Ambition and freedom were never the trade you were told
they were, and AI is how you stop choosing.

### Guardrails

**Tone summary.** Direct, generous, data-forward, warm, and unimpressed by hype.

**What the brand cannot be.**

- A guru. No revelation framing, no "the truth nobody is telling you."
- A hype account. No doom forecasting, no "this changes everything."
- Corporate. No jargon without explanation, no hedged non-statements.
- A gatekeeper. Never assume technical background or make the reader feel behind.
- Hustle culture. Never frame exhaustion as commitment.
- Dishonest by omission. Never recommend an untested tool or hide a limitation.

**Litmus test.** If Sam couldn't run it tonight, it isn't finished. If it sounds
like a guru, it's wrong.

## Voice

### Identity

I'm the friend who is a few steps ahead and takes notes for you. I did the
whole prestige track, the scholarship, the big-tech product job, the
venture-backed company, the exit, and I came out the other side convinced most
of it was optional. Now I build a small business with my husband and teach the
AI workflows that made it possible for two people to do the work of a team.

I talk to you like a peer who happens to have run the experiment already. I'll
give you the exact prompt, the exact tool, the exact number of hours it saved,
and I'll tell you when something didn't work, because a recommendation that has
never failed anyone isn't a recommendation. I don't need you to be technical. I
do need you to actually try it.

What I care about is the line between the two halves: the AI move on one side,
the time it buys you on the other. Anything that skips the second half is just
software content.

**Essence.** A smart friend who figured something out and can't wait to hand it
to you, with the receipts attached.

### Message Pillars

**1. Time-rich.**
Ambition and freedom are not a trade. Being rich in hours is the actual goal,
and money that costs you your life is a worse deal than it looks.

**2. Practical AI.**
Workflows over theory. A prompt you can paste, a tool you can open, a result you
can see tonight. If it can't be run, it isn't taught.

**3. Proof and receipts.**
Real numbers, real screenshots, real failures. Borrowed credibility is fine when
it's cited. Invented credibility is never fine.

**4. Access.**
No CS degree, no technical co-founder, no network required. The people who most
need AI to work for them are the ones nobody is writing for.

**5. The freedom game.**
The prestige track is one option, chosen rather than assumed. Optionality is
built deliberately, and staying is a valid choice when it's a choice.

**6. Small on purpose.**
Two people, bootstrapped, in public. Output per person over headcount, and the
real numbers shared along the way.

### Tonal Rules

1. Write like a smart friend who figured something out and is excited to share
   it. Peer to peer, never teacher to student.
2. Open with tension the reader already feels. A hook that gives away the answer
   has no reason to be read.
3. Be specific to the point of discomfort. Exact tool, exact prompt, exact hours
   saved, exact dollar amount. Vagueness reads as never having done it.
4. Draw the line to freedom. Every AI move connects to the time or optionality
   it produces. Stopping at the tactic is half the job.
5. Spiky claims are welcome, but back them with data, logic, or lived
   experience. A spiky claim with nothing under it is a hype account.
6. Say what didn't work. Gaps, limitations, and failed attempts are content.
7. Assume no technical background, and never make that assumption feel like a
   concession.
8. Match register to format: brief and operational for captions, emails, and
   outreach; structured and scannable for guides and strategy docs; warmer and
   more expressive for reflective or personal writing.
9. Use analogies to introduce anything new. Concepts land through comparison.
10. Every piece ends with one clear call to action. Never more than one.
11. Avoid em dashes. Use the Oxford comma sparingly.
12. No sycophantic openers, no filler affirmations, no padding, no summary the
    reader didn't ask for.

**Identity boundaries: what we are not.**

- We are not a news source. We do not cover model releases as events.
- We are not motivational. We do not sell a feeling without a method.
- We are not neutral. We take positions and show the reasoning.
- We are not exhaustive. One idea per piece, finished properly.
- We are not selling escape. Staying is a legitimate outcome when it's chosen.

**Forbidden patterns.** These read as machine-written and are banned regardless
of how well they perform elsewhere.

- Negation reframe: "It's not just X, it's Y."
- Dramatic contrast: "It's not automation, it's amplification."
- Philosophical reduction: "AI isn't replacing people, it's replacing waiting."
- Revelation hook: "Here's the truth no one is talking about."
- Elliptical setup: "The best part? You don't have to lift a finger."
- Fragmented questions manufacturing false intrigue.
- Generic hot takes: "95% of people use AI wrong."

| We Say | We Never Say |
|---|---|
| "This saved me 6 hours last week" | "This will 10x your productivity" |
| "Here's the exact prompt" | "Here's the secret nobody tells you" |
| "I tried it and it didn't work" | "This changes everything" |
| "You don't need to be a developer" | "Even beginners can do this" |
| "Time-rich" | "Living your best life" |
| "A workflow" | "A game-changing hack" |
| "Here's what it cost" | "Unlock your full potential" |
| "Two people, no ad spend" | "We're disrupting the space" |

## Visual

### Logo & Marks

The mark is a monochrome wordmark, black on white, held at
`public/mika-reyes-logo.png`. It carries no color of its own, which is
deliberate: the identity's color comes from the work around the mark, not the
mark itself.

**Invariants.** Never recolor it outside Ink or pure white. Never place it on a
busy photograph without a solid ground behind it. Never stretch, rotate, add
effects, or enclose it in a shape that isn't part of the file. Clear space on
all sides is at least the cap height of the wordmark.

Placement, responsive sizing, and whether a given surface shows the mark at all
are surface decisions and belong in that surface's `DESIGN.md`. The deck, for
instance, legitimately shows the `@its.mikareyes` handle instead.

### Core Colors

- **Ink** `#111111` (mandatory). Nearly all type, on nearly every surface. Not
  pure black, so it reads as considered rather than default.
- **Coral** `#E8425A` (mandatory). The single point of heat. It marks the one
  thing that matters on a screen: the word being emphasized, the action being
  asked for. Its meaning depends on scarcity, so more of it is always worse.
- **Teal** `#0E8C8C` (optional). The counterweight. Used where coral would be
  too loud or would compete with itself, and for the calm, secondary register.
- **White** `#FFFFFF` (mandatory). The ground. Space is part of the identity,
  not what's left over.

**Avoid:** purple and violet of any kind, previously carried in the tokens and
now retired. Neon. Gradients used as decoration. Dark-mode-first palettes, which
belong to the developer-tools category this brand is not in.

Roles are not assigned here. That coral is the call-to-action color on the
website and the highlight color in the decks are decisions each surface makes in
its own `DESIGN.md`.

> **Migration note (version 1).** The deck system historically used
> `#fd4869` as `--deck-accent`, a near-duplicate of Coral. Coral `#E8425A` is
> now the single approved red. `src/app/deck/deck.css` should be repointed, and
> `--mr-coral-bright` `#FF3D68` is a hover state, not an identity color: it
> belongs in the website `DESIGN.md`.

### Typefaces

Two approved voices, split by surface. That split is deliberate: a page you
read and a slide glimpsed for four seconds beside a talking head are different
jobs, and one face cannot be optimal at both.

- **Bricolage Grotesque**, the brand voice **on the website**. Every headline
  and every display moment there. Its slight irregularity is the point:
  confident and a little warm rather than neutral and corporate. Fallback:
  `ui-sans-serif, system-ui, -apple-system, sans-serif`. Licensing: SIL Open
  Font License 1.1. Verified.
- **Inter**, the brand voice **on the presentation decks**. Chosen where type is
  set enormous and read fast: its neutrality is an advantage at 128px, where
  Bricolage's character starts competing with the message, and its optical size
  axis keeps the letterforms right from a small credit to a full-bleed hero.
  Fallback: `ui-sans-serif, system-ui, -apple-system, sans-serif`. Licensing:
  SIL Open Font License 1.1. Verified.
- **Hanken Grotesk**, body and long-form reading on the website. Chosen to sit
  quietly under Bricolage without competing with it. Fallback:
  `ui-sans-serif, system-ui, -apple-system, sans-serif`. Licensing: SIL Open
  Font License 1.1. Verified.
- **Geist Mono**, numbers, metadata, step markers, and anything that should read
  as data rather than prose. Fallback: `ui-monospace, monospace`. Licensing:
  SIL Open Font License 1.1. Verified.

Neither voice crosses into the other's surface. A deck set in Bricolage or a
web page set in Inter is off-brand, not a variation.

Sizes, weights, line heights, and tracking are a type scale and belong to each
surface's `DESIGN.md`.

### Photography & Illustration

**Mood.** Real, specific, warm, unstyled, present-tense.

**Subjects.** Screenshots of the actual tool with the actual result on screen.
Photographs of Mika that look like a person working, not a person posing. The
specific person or company being discussed, shown rather than described. Press
logos and coverage as cited evidence. Charts and diagrams when the argument is
quantitative.

**Avoid.** Stock photography of any kind. Abstract AI imagery: glowing brains,
blue circuit boards, humanoid robots, neural-network wallpaper. Anything
suggesting a product or result that doesn't exist. Faces of real people used
decoratively rather than because the piece is about them.

**Illustration.** Diagrams are illustration here, and they are built rather than
drawn: explicit geometry, labeled, on-brand, doing argumentative work. Emoji are
the sanctioned lightweight icon system and are used as objects on the page, not
as punctuation inside sentences.

The rule underneath all of it: a real screenshot beats a good illustration, and
a good illustration beats a stock photograph every time.

### Art Direction

**Design keywords.** Oversized type. Generous space. One point of heat.
Receipts-first. Warm ground. Deliberate play. Legible across a room.

**Direction statement.** The identity should feel like being handed something
useful by someone who already did the work. Every surface makes one thing
obviously the most important thing, and everything else gets out of its way.

**Visual territory.** A field guide printed on warm paper: headings large enough
to read from across the room, one red pen reserved for what actually matters,
real screenshots pasted in as evidence, and a few stickers in the margins
because the person who made it was enjoying themselves.

Each surface narrows this rather than repeating it. The deck is the same field
guide held up at arm's length: one idea per page, type at its largest, nothing
that survives being seen for four seconds on video.

## Governance

### Naming

Guides, decks, and posts are named for what the reader gets, in the reader's
words, not for the concept being taught. "How to Give Claude Memory" rather than
"Persistent Context Architecture."

URLs are lowercase kebab-case and describe the content, not the format. Slugs
are permanent once published; renaming requires a redirect.

**Reserved.** *time-rich* and *the freedom game* are brand-owned framings and
should not be used casually to mean something else.

**Requires approval before shipping:** any new tagline, any new named framework
or method, and any use of the wordmark outside mikareyes.com surfaces.

**Prohibited, never publish.**

- Any result, saving, or outcome that has not actually happened.

An AI agent may draft candidate claims but must label them as drafts. It must
never move a claim into the approved table without a human doing so.

### Accessibility Commitments

Motion is never required to understand anything. Every animated surface honors
`prefers-reduced-motion`, and the reduced state is a real state rather than a
degraded one. This is already implemented in `src/app/globals.css` and
`src/app/deck/deck.css`, and it applies to anything added later.

Type carries meaning at legible sizes. No message-bearing text is set below a
surface's established floor, and color is never the only thing distinguishing
one piece of information from another.

Contrast targets, focus treatments, and the per-component measurements that
verify them belong to each surface's `DESIGN.md`. This section is the
commitment; that is where it gets checked.
