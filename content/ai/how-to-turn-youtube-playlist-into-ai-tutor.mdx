You've got YouTube playlists of marketing tutorials and growth hacks you never finish. Paste one into NotebookLM, pull out the framework, then drop it into Gemini Canvas and make a tutor you actually click through. Under five minutes. Free Google tools you already have.

I do this when I want to sharpen taste and creativity, not collect more "watch later" guilt. Passive rewatching gets you about 10% retention. Working the material gets you closer to 75%.

![Flow diagram showing a YouTube playlist moving through NotebookLM and Gemini into an AI tutor](/ai-guides/youtube-playlist-ai-tutor-flow.png "YouTube playlist → NotebookLM → Gemini → AI tutor")

### Why this matters

Saving a playlist feels productive. Finishing it rarely is. You watch the same video twice, remember nothing useful, and still can't teach the framework back. NotebookLM reads the video for you. Gemini Canvas turns the outline into something you quiz yourself on. Same content, way stickier.

If you've already built an [AI coach in Claude](/ai/how-to-create-an-ai-coach), this is the YouTube version of that move: load the source, then build a place to practice.

### **1. Paste the YouTube link into NotebookLM**

Open [NotebookLM](https://notebooklm.google.com) and make a new notebook. Paste the playlist or single video URL as a source. NotebookLM reads the whole thing. No transcript download. No cleanup.

I used a video on unlocking creative taste, mostly because as a growth and marketing cofounder I keep needing that muscle. Within a minute the source was loaded and a summary of the core ideas was sitting in chat.

![NotebookLM chat summarizing a creativity video with Studio learning formats on the right](/ai-guides/youtube-playlist-ai-tutor-notebooklm.png "NotebookLM after pasting the YouTube source: summary on the left, Studio formats on the right")

### **2. Pull the framework, then explore other formats**

Ask NotebookLM for the core framework the video teaches, then squeeze it into a short outline. Keep it tight: the principles, the order, and what you'd actually practice.

Then poke around Studio. Flashcards, quizzes, mind maps, audio overviews, slide decks. You don't need all of them. Pick whatever matches how you learn.

Prompts I use:

```text
Pull out the core framework this video teaches in 5–7 steps.
```

```text
Turn that into a short study outline I can practice against.
```

```text
What would a 10-question quiz on this look like?
```

> **Key insight:** NotebookLM extracts. Gemini is where you practice. Don't make one tool do both.

### **3. Build an interactive tutor in Gemini Canvas**

Take the outline into Gemini (bring the NotebookLM source along if Gemini can see it). Turn on Canvas, then ask it to build a simple interactive app so you're working the material instead of rewatching it.

Prompt that worked for me:

```text
Develop an app from the attached notebook that teaches me this topic. Use Canvas as the tool.
```

Gemini built me a creativity tutor with an overview, a concept journey, a mentor chat, and a quiz. That's when it clicked: I wasn't "reviewing" anymore. I was using it.

![Gemini interface with the Canvas option open and NotebookLM linked at the top](/ai-guides/youtube-playlist-ai-tutor-gemini-canvas.png "In Gemini, open the tools menu and choose Canvas before you send the build prompt")

![Gemini Canvas preview of The 365-Day Creative Studio tutor app with Overview, Mentor Chat, and Challenge Arena](/ai-guides/youtube-playlist-ai-tutor-final-app.png "The finished tutor: overview, interactive journey, mentor chat, and quiz in one Canvas app")

### What changes when you learn actively?

Rewatching a growth video feels like progress. Quizzing yourself on the framework actually is. Once Gemini builds the tutor, you stop consuming and start rehearsing. Overview, concept checks, ask-anything practice, a diagnostic quiz. Whatever gets you saying the ideas out loud.

![Bar chart comparing roughly 10% retention from passive watching vs 75% from active use](/ai-guides/youtube-playlist-ai-tutor-retention.png "Passive watching ~10%. Active use ~75%.")

This works for more than marketing playlists. I've used it on sales frameworks and product teardown series too. Anything you keep saving and never finishing.
