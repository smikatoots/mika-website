When I was a PM at LinkedIn launching the "I'm Hiring" profile ring, design never walked into review with a single mock. They brought a wall of them, and the conversation was about which one won, not whether the one existed. Working alone without a designer, that used to be impossible. Now it costs one prompt.

## Why this matters

The best creative marketers and designers I know never bring one design to a review. They bring ten and argue their way to the best one. You can do the same thing alone, in about five minutes, by uploading a brand file to Paper or Claude Design and asking for directions instead of a design.

You already do this with words. You would never write one headline for your landing page and ship it. You write ten and pick. Your graphics deserve the same treatment.

The prompt is short and the whole thing is repeatable. Here is the exact workflow.

## Step 1: Make a brand file

Start with a brand.md holding your colors, your fonts, and the feel you are going for. Without it, ten options come back in ten unrelated styles and every one of them is unusable.

![A BRAND.md file open in an editor showing YAML frontmatter with name, tagline, and version, followed by Strategy, Overview, Promise, and Guardrails sections](/ai-guides/brand-md-file-example.png "The brand file is what makes ten options read as ten versions of your brand rather than ten random designs.")

[Here is the full guide to creating a BRAND.md file](/ai/how-to-create-a-brand-md-file) if you do not have one yet. It takes about ten minutes with a free plugin.

## Step 2: Open Paper or Claude Design

Upload your brand file to whichever tool you are using. Claude Design works fine and is convenient if you already have it open. Paper is a design canvas built for an agent to work inside, so it handles the "make ten of these" request more naturally.

I like to use Paper over Claude Design, Canva or Figma because:

- Using Claude Design eats through my tokens fast
- The Paper and Claude connection works SO WELL! I find that the Figma and Canva MCPs kind of suck

If you want the full Paper setup, [the carousel guide walks through connecting it to Claude](/ai/how-to-make-instagram-carousels-with-paper).

## Step 3: Give it the raw material

Drop in the actual text and the actual photo you plan to use into a canvas. Or, if you already have a carousel or certain base design, you can use that, too (though there might be restrictions on what Paper is able to give as options).

## Step 4: Ask for directions, not a design

On Paper, select the assets you want to use. Then on Claude, use this starter prompt and update as needed:

```
I'm building graphics for my business. Here's the text and photo that I want
to use. Use my brand guidelines to come up with 10 different design options I can
choose from.
```

I used the 1st graphic below to generate 4 different design directions.

![A Paper canvas showing a source graphic on the left and four labeled design directions beside it: stat spike, tension plus cost, proof or receipt, and direct instruction, each using the same photo and brand colors](/ai-guides/design-options-paper-directions.png "Four of the directions from one run, each labeled by angle. Same photo, same brand, completely different message strategy.")

One leads with a statistic, one with tension and cost, one with proof, one with direct instruction. Same photo, same brand, four different reasons to stop scrolling.

## Step 5: Pick one, then iterate

Choose your favorite and ask for five more versions of just that direction, or start editing it directly. Paper makes it nice and easy! The second round is where the design gets good, because you are refining something that already works instead of exploring from zero.

## Step 6: Save it as a skill

If you do this weekly, package the whole thing into a Claude skill so it becomes one command instead of a prompt you rewrite each time. The skill holds the instructions, your brand rules, and the ask-for-ten structure, so you go straight to reviewing options.
