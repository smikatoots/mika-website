You can spot an AI-made poster in about a second, even when you can't say why. The tell is almost always the same thing: too many inconsistent details competing for your attention at once.

I know because I spent the start of my career in product design, so my eye is trained to catch what feels off. Your audience catches it too, they just can't name it. That unnamed "something is wrong here" feeling is what costs you trust before anyone reads a single word.

Good design is invisible. When every choice lines up, people feel calm and they believe you. When the choices fight each other, people feel a low hum of doubt, and doubt is expensive when you are trying to sell, teach, or get someone to follow you. Here are the six tells I look for, with real examples, so you can catch them in your own work before you hit post.

## How do you tell if a poster was made with AI?

Run through these six checks. Most AI posters trip at least three of them, and once you see the pattern you can't unsee it.

**1. There's no white space.** White space is intentional emptiness, and it's what lets a design breathe. AI fills every pixel because it has nothing telling it to stop, so the result feels crowded and chaotic. If your eye doesn't know where to rest, that's the first tell.

![AI-generated Ginger and Tall charity poster packed edge to edge with flowers, balloons, props, and people, leaving no white space](/ai-guides/ai-poster-icks-ginger-and-tall.png "No white space: every inch is packed, so your eye has nowhere to land.")

**2. There's too much information in tiny text.** AI loves to pack in a full paragraph and then shrink it to fit. You end up squinting at an infographic to read anything. A human designer cuts words so the important ones can be big. AI keeps them all and makes them small.

![AI-made How to Build Trust infographic with a five-layer diagram beside a dense before-and-after table set in small text](/ai-guides/ai-poster-icks-build-trust.png "Too much crammed in: the before-and-after table repeats a full line of tiny text five times over.")

**3. The fonts don't match.** Look at how many different typefaces are fighting on one AI poster, in different sizes and different families, with no logic to which is which. A real brand uses two, maybe three fonts on purpose. AI mixes five because each one looked good on its own.

![AI-generated Sam's Club birthday flyer mixing a script font, a brush font, and several block fonts in one design](/ai-guides/ai-poster-icks-sams-club.png "Mismatched fonts: a script 'Happy,' a brush 'Birthday,' and several block fonts with no logic to which is which.")

**4. The art styles don't match.** This one is the fastest giveaway. On the live comedy poster below, the "every Thursday 8pm" block is a flat vector style, the microphone is rendered in realistic 3D, and "LIVE COMEDY" is rough textured type. Three different worlds glued into one frame. A designer picks one visual language and commits.

![AI-generated Live Comedy Night poster mixing a flat vector layout, a photoreal 3D microphone, and rough textured lettering in one image](/ai-guides/ai-poster-icks-live-comedy.png "Clashing styles: a realistic 3D mic next to flat vector text next to gritty texture type.")

**5. The details fall apart when you look closely.** Zoom in and AI shows its hands, literally. Warped fingers, melted letters, faces that are almost right but land in the uncanny valley. The poster reads fine from a scroll, then breaks the moment someone actually looks.

![AI-generated book cover reading Unleash Your Creativity With AI with an ornate portrait whose hands and features look off up close](/ai-guides/ai-poster-icks-99-midjourney.png "Uncanny details: look closely at the hands and face and the AI rendering starts to break down.")

**6. It has AI's identity, not yours.** Before anyone reads a word or spots your logo, they should unconsciously know a design came from you. The posters above actually do have a strong identity, it's just AI's identity, not the creator's. Anything that makes your work yours gets flattened into the same generic look everyone else's prompts produce.

> **Key insight:** AI doesn't have taste, it has an average. Every poster it makes carries its identity, not yours.

## Why does AI design look off even when it's detailed?

Because detail is not the same as design. AI is very good at generating more, and design is mostly about deciding what to remove. The same instinct shows up in text, which is why [AI writing has its own tells you can learn to strip out](/ai/humanizer). Once you train your eye on the visual version, you start applying the same edit everywhere: fewer fonts, more space, one consistent style, and a [color palette that actually belongs together](/ai/color-theory-analysis). That discipline is also what keeps [your own social graphics on-brand](/ai/carousel-generator) instead of looking like everyone else's.

None of this means AI is useless for design. It means raw AI output is a starting point, not a finished poster.
